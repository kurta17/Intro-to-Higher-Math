Session 4 · Polynomials, exponentials, logarithms, and trigonometric models
Set Thu 10 Sep 2026

Part A · Mechanics

1Polynomial shape
Let $p(x)=-x^2+2x+3$.
  1. State the degree and leading coefficient of $p$.
  2. Describe what the graph does at its left and right ends.
  3. Sketch the graph. Give the intercepts with both axes and the vertex, and say how you found each one.
Solution (a) The degree is $2$ and the leading coefficient is $-1$.
(b) The degree is even and the leading coefficient is negative, so the graph goes down at both ends: $p(x)\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$ and as $x\to\infty$.
(c) $x$-intercepts: factor. $-x^2+2x+3=-(x^2-2x-3)=-(x+1)(x-3)$, so $p(x)=0$ exactly when $x=-1$ or $x=3$, giving $(-1,0)$ and $(3,0)$.
$y$-intercept: set $x=0$, so $p(0)=3$ and the point is $(0,3)$.
Vertex: complete the square, $-x^2+2x+3=-(x-1)^2+4$. Since $(x-1)^2\geq0$ with equality only at $x=1$, the maximum is $4$ at $x=1$: the vertex is $(1,4)$. (Equivalently $x=-\tfrac{b}{2a}=-\tfrac{2}{-2}=1$, then $p(1)=4$.)
The sketch is a downward parabola through $(-1,0)$, $(0,3)$, $(3,0)$, peaking at $(1,4)$.
2Quadratic transformation
Start with $f(x)=x^2$. The function $$g(x)=-(x-2)^2+4$$ is made from $f$.
  1. Describe the shift and reflection.
  2. Give the vertex and find the real roots of $g$.
Solution Move the graph of $y=x^2$ right by $2$, reflect it in the $x$-axis, then move it up by $4$. Its vertex is $(2,4)$. For roots, $-(x-2)^2+4=0\Longleftrightarrow(x-2)^2=4\Longleftrightarrow x-2=\pm2$, so $x=0$ or $x=4$.
3Exponential equation
Solve $$5^{2x}-30\cdot5^{x}+125=0.$$ Give all real solutions and justify each step.
Solution Put $u=5^{x}$. Then $5^{2x}=(5^{x})^{2}=u^{2}$, and the equation becomes the quadratic $$u^{2}-30u+125=0.$$ The substitution is legitimate because $5^{x}$ is defined for every real $x$, and it takes only positive values — which matters when we come back at the end.
Factoring, $u^{2}-30u+125=(u-25)(u-5)$, so $u=25$ or $u=5$. Both are positive, so each gives a genuine solution.
Returning to $x$: since $5^{t}$ is one-to-one (the base $5$ is positive and not $1$), $$5^{x}=25=5^{2}\Longrightarrow x=2,\qquad 5^{x}=5=5^{1}\Longrightarrow x=1.$$ The real solutions are $x=2$ and $x=1$.
Check. At $x=2$: $625-750+125=0$. At $x=1$: $25-150+125=0$. ✓
4Change of base
Solve exactly for $x$: $$\log_2(x-1)+\log_4(x-1)=6.$$ Use the change-of-base formula, and state the domain restriction before solving.
Solution Domain first. Both logarithms take the input $x-1$, which must be positive, so $x-1>0$, that is $x>1$. Everything below is carried out on that domain.
Change of base. Writing $\log_4 t=\dfrac{\log_2 t}{\log_2 4}=\dfrac{\log_2 t}{2}$ puts both terms in base $2$. With $t=x-1$ the equation becomes $$\log_2(x-1)+\tfrac12\log_2(x-1)=6\Longleftrightarrow\tfrac32\log_2(x-1)=6 \Longleftrightarrow\log_2(x-1)=4.$$ Hence $x-1=2^4=16$, so $x=17$, which satisfies $x>1$.
Check. $\log_2 16+\log_4 16=4+2=6$. ✓

Part B · Reasoning and modelling

5Logarithmic equation
Solve over the real numbers: $$\log_2(x-1)+\log_2(x+1)=3.$$ State the domain before combining the logarithms. Check every possible answer in the original equation.
Solution Both logarithms require positive inputs: $x-1>0$ and $x+1>0$. Therefore $x>1$. On this domain, the product law gives $$\log_2\bigl((x-1)(x+1)\bigr)=3\Longleftrightarrow x^2-1=8 \Longleftrightarrow x^2=9.$$ The algebra gives $x=3$ or $x=-3$, but the domain keeps only $x=3$. Check: $\log_2(2)+\log_2(4)=1+2=3$.
6Growth comparison
Put the following functions in order from slowest growth to fastest growth as the positive integer $n$ becomes very large: $$\log_2 n, \qquad n, \qquad n\log_2 n, \qquad n^2, \qquad 2^n.$$ In two or three sentences, explain why checking one value such as $n=3$ does not prove the full ranking.
Solution The eventual order is $$\log_2 n < n < n\log_2 n < n^2 < 2^n.$$ This is an eventual statement: it becomes true once $n$ is large enough, not necessarily at every positive integer. For example, at $n=3$, $2^n=8<9=n^2$, but for sufficiently large $n$ the exponential $2^n$ is larger than $n^2$.
7Periodic model
Let $t$ be the number of hours after midnight. A simple model for temperature is $$T(t)=3\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{6}(t-1)\right)+20 \qquad (0\leq t\leq12).$$
  1. State the amplitude, midline, period, and range.
  2. At what time does the model first reach a maximum? At what time does it reach a minimum?
  3. Explain why $(t-1)$ moves the graph right by $1$ hour, rather than left by $1$ hour.
Solution The amplitude is $3$, the midline is $y=20$, the period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{\pi/6}=12$ hours, and the range is $[17,23]$. A maximum occurs when the sine input is $\pi/2$, so $\frac{\pi}{6}(t-1)=\frac\pi2$ gives $t=4$. A minimum occurs when the input is $3\pi/2$, giving $t=10$. In $f(t-1)$, the new input must be $t=1$ to give the old value $f(0)$, so the graph moves right by $1$.

Part C · Communicate precisely

8Find the error
A student writes: “$\log_2(u+v)=\log_2u+\log_2v$ because logarithms turn an operation into addition.”
  1. Identify the false statement.
  2. Give a counterexample with positive $u$ and $v$.
  3. Write the correct logarithm law for a product, including its condition.
Solution The false statement is $\log_2(u+v)=\log_2u+\log_2v$. Take $u=v=1$: $\log_2(1+1)=\log_2 2=1$, while $\log_2 1+\log_2 1=0+0=0$. The correct product law is $\log_2(uv)=\log_2u+\log_2v$ for $u>0$ and $v>0$.
9Make a claim precise
The statement “An exponential function is always bigger than a polynomial” is false or unclear as written. Rewrite it as a true statement about $c^n$ and $n^k$, including all important conditions. Then give one numerical example showing why the words “for sufficiently large $n$” are necessary.
Solution One correct version is: “For every fixed $c>1$ and $k>0$, there is a positive integer $N$ such that $c^n>n^k$ for every integer $n\geq N$.” The word “fixed” matters: the bases and powers are not changing with $n$. For $c=2$, $k=2$, and $n=3$, we have $2^3=8<9=3^2$, so the inequality is not true for every positive $n$.
